The shiftThe questions you used to buy on Google are now answered by AI

When a couple asks ChatGPT “how do we adopt a newborn?”, it doesn't return ten blue links. It answers — names three or four organizations, cites its sources, and moves on. There is no keyword bid into that answer. The organic discipline that replaces PPC-for-discovery is called AEO — and unlike most marketing, it's directly measurable.

What's actually buyable in AI ads today

Verified as of July 2026: OpenAI began testing ads in ChatGPT (US) in February 2026 — shown only to free-tier users, clearly labeled, and per OpenAI they do not influence the organic answer. There is no open self-serve ad platform yet. Google's AI Mode carries ads through regular Google Ads. Perplexity reportedly discontinued its ad program over user-trust concerns. Bottom line: paid placement covers a sliver of AI answers; the main game is being the name in the organic answer.

Full methodology — and its honest limits

I wrote 30 questions phrased the way real people ask assistants — adoptive-parent questions (how to adopt, agency vs attorney, costs, local searches near Orange County, LA, Dallas, Phoenix), birth-mother questions (options, financial support, placement), and brand checks (“is Adoption Network Law Center legitimate?”). Each question went to ChatGPT and Gemini with web search enabled, twice, to account for answer variability — 120 answers total, collected 2026-07-05.

Each answer was scored: was Adoption Network Law Center named? recommended? which competitors appeared? which websites did the AI cite as sources? The brand-name check is a literal text match, not an AI's opinion — I verified the headline numbers by hand.

Limits, stated plainly: answers came through the models' developer interfaces with web search on — the closest measurable proxy to the consumer apps, but not byte-identical (no personal chat history). Answers also shift between runs and over time; that's why the plan includes re-measuring monthly rather than trusting any single snapshot — including this one.

Finding 2The AIs read your website. They almost never credit you.

Here's the part I didn't expect. adoptionnetwork.com is the #2 most-cited source across all 120 answers — 45 citations. The AIs are reading your content to write these answers. 29 answers were built on your material; 2 credited you by name.

That's the diagnosis in one sentence: a credit problem, not a content problem. Your content earns retrieval; your brand entity is too weak for attribution. And a credit problem is fixable.

Finding 4Birth mothers get deflected; adoptive parents are winnable now

On 46% of birth-mother questions, the AIs deliberately avoided naming any provider and pointed to neutral resources instead — a built-in safety behavior nobody beats head-on. The near-term battleground is adoptive-parent visibility; the birth-mother path runs through presence on the neutral resources the AIs do cite.

One more thing you should see: when someone asks the AIs whether to trust Adoption Network Law Center, the answer today is mixed — praise alongside concern narratives. Reputation is part of this fight, and it's measured monthly like everything else in the plan.
