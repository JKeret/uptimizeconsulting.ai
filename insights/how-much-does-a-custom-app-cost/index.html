How much does a custom app for a small business cost?

Short answer: a useful custom app starts around $1,000 for a single automated process, a full business platform typically runs $10,000 to $25,000, and ongoing care is about $1,500 per month. You do not need to start at the top of that range. You should not.

I build custom apps for small businesses, and this is the question I get first in almost every conversation. The honest answer is that "custom app" covers everything from a two-week automation to a system that runs your whole operation, so here are the real tiers I quote:

Starter
$1,000

One process automated in about two weeks. Fixed price.

Platform
$10,000 to $25,000

A system your team runs the business on, built in phases.

Care
$1,500/mo

Hosting, changes, and support once the software matters.

What actually drives the price

Three things move a project from the starter tier toward the platform tier:

  • Integrations. Software that must talk to your billing system, your CRM, or an insurance clearinghouse costs more than software that stands alone.
  • Compliance. If the app touches health, financial, or legal data, protecting it properly is real work and priced accordingly.
  • Roles. One shared screen is cheap. Separate views for owners, staff, and customers multiply the surface area.

A real example

A home-care agency came to me buried in insurance paperwork. We did not start with a platform. We started with a $1,000 project: an app that reads claim statements and flags billing errors automatically. It worked, so we kept going, phase by phase. That first project grew into a platform that now runs their billing compliance end to end. The point of the story is the order: working software first, big commitment later.

Can I start small?

Yes, and you should. Pick the process that wastes the most of your week. A starter project proves in two weeks what a proposal can only promise. If the software earns its keep, expand it. If it does not, you spent $1,000 to find out, not tens of thousands.

What does $1,000 actually buy?

One working piece of software that removes one manual task: an app that reads documents and flags errors, a form that replaces a paper workflow, a report that builds itself every Monday. We scope it in a 30-minute call, I build it in about two weeks, and the price is fixed before I write a line of code.
