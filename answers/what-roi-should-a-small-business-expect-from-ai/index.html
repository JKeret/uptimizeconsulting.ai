What ROI should a small business expect from AI?

For one scoped automation, expect payback in one to three months if it removes a real, measured chunk of someone's paid time. I'm Jonathan at Uptimize Consulting, and I'd rather run that simple math with you on a call than quote you an enterprise study that has nothing to do with your business.

Search this question and you'll find IBM reporting only about a quarter of AI initiatives deliver expected ROI, Deloitte putting typical payback at two to four years, and MIT research finding most enterprise generative AI projects show no measurable financial return within six months. Every one of those numbers describes company-wide AI transformation programs costing hundreds of thousands of dollars, with governance committees and adoption campaigns. That's not what a $1,000 automation is, and it's not the ROI question you should be asking.

Why the enterprise numbers don't apply to you

Those studies are measuring whether a Fortune 500 company's multi-year AI strategy paid off across dozens of departments. The failure modes they're counting, poor adoption, unclear ownership, projects that never left the pilot stage, mostly come from scale and organizational complexity. A single automated process, scoped by the person who actually runs it, doesn't carry that same risk.

The math that actually matters

Take the hours per month the task currently costs, multiply by what that person's time is worth per hour, and compare that monthly number to the project price. If a task takes five hours a week at $25 an hour, that's roughly $500 a month in labor. A $1,000 project pays for itself in two months and keeps paying after that.

Here's how the math works on a typical case

This is an illustrative example, not a specific client's numbers, but it's a realistic one. Say a weekly reporting process takes about two hours every Monday morning, at a fully loaded cost of around $40 an hour, that's roughly $320 a month in labor. A $1,000 automation replacing that process pays for itself around month three, and every month after that is time back with nothing more to pay. That's the entire ROI conversation for a project like this: not a percentage, a payback period you can say out loud.

When ROI doesn't show up

Two situations reliably kill ROI on an automation project. The process wasn't actually costing much time to begin with, so the math never closes even though it felt like a hassle. Or the process changes so often that the automation needs constant rework, which quietly turns a one-time cost into an ongoing one. ROI depends far more on picking the right process than on the AI itself, which is why picking the first process to automate is the question that actually determines the outcome here.
