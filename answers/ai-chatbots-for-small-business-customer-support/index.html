Should I use an AI chatbot for customer support?

Yes, for the routine stuff: hours, pricing, order status, booking a first appointment. Not for the whole job. A chatbot that handles the repetitive front layer and hands off everything else to a person is what actually works. I'm Jonathan at Uptimize Consulting, and I've built this both ways, and the narrow version is the one clients keep using.

The general answers describe chatbots as a near-total customer support solution: 24/7 availability, instant responses, personalized recommendations, proactive engagement. All true in theory. In practice, for a business with one or two support people, the value is much more specific: it's catching the questions that don't need a human at all, so the humans have time for the ones that do.

What it should handle

  • Hours, location, and basic info. The questions that get asked ten times a day with the exact same answer every time.
  • Order or appointment status. "Where's my order" or "when's my appointment" answered instantly instead of someone digging through a system.
  • Initial booking. Getting a new customer's details and preferred time captured, even at midnight, so a person just has to confirm it in the morning.

What it shouldn't handle

Complaints, refund decisions, and anything with real emotion behind it need a person, not a script pretending to be sympathetic. A good chatbot recognizes when a question doesn't have a clean answer and says "let me get someone for you" instead of guessing its way through a frustrated customer. The failure mode I actively design against is a bot that sounds confident while being wrong.

Build versus buy

A generic SaaS chatbot widget, the kind you can install in an afternoon, is fine if all it needs to do is answer FAQ questions from a script. It stops being enough the moment you need it to check a real order status, look at your actual appointment calendar, or answer from your specific knowledge base instead of generic training. That's when a custom build, tied into your systems, is worth the money over a subscription that never quite fits.

What this costs

A chatbot scoped to answer FAQs and capture leads is a solid $1,000 starter project. One that needs to check live order status or booking availability across a real system runs closer to the platform tier, in the $10,000 to $25,000 range covered on what AI automation costs.
