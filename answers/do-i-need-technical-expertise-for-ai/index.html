Do I need technical expertise to use AI in my business?

No. You don't need technical skills to use AI tools yourself, and you don't need them to get a custom automation built into your business either, because building it is my job. I'm Jonathan at Uptimize Consulting, and the most technical thing I need from a client is a clear description of how their process works today.

The general answers online are correct as far as they go: using ChatGPT or Gemini day to day takes no coding, while building custom integrations does take real technical skill. What they skip is the part that actually matters to you, which is that "building custom integrations" describes what I do for you, not a skill you need to develop yourself.

Two different questions hiding in one

"Do I need technical expertise for AI" usually means one of two things. If it means "can I personally use an AI tool without learning to code," the answer is yes, anyone can open ChatGPT and start typing. If it means "can I have a custom automation built into my invoicing, my CRM, my scheduling," the answer is also yes, but that's a project you hire out, not a skill you personally need.

What you don't need to know

  • How to write code or read an API document.
  • What a large language model is or how it works under the hood.
  • Anything about hosting, databases, or software architecture.

None of that is your job in this relationship. It's mine.

What you do need to bring

You need to know your own process well enough to describe it out loud: what happens first, what happens next, and what the weird exceptions are that come up once a month. That's not technical knowledge, it's business knowledge, and you already have it because you run the business. The scoping call is mostly me asking questions and you answering them in plain language.

When it's worth learning some AI yourself

Separate from having something custom built, spending an afternoon learning to use ChatGPT or Gemini for drafting emails, summarizing documents, or brainstorming is worth it for almost any owner, and it costs nothing but time. That's a different thing from hiring someone to wire AI into your systems, which is what a $1,000 starter project actually is.

And if the task you're weighing is genuinely small, a five-minute job done twice a month, don't hire anyone, technical or not. Tools like Zapier or Make are built for non-developers and will get you further than you'd expect on simple, single-step tasks without paying a consultant at all. Come to me once the process is bigger than a no-code tool can comfortably handle.
