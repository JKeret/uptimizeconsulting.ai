How do I choose an AI consultant in Chicago?

Ask what specific process they'd automate first, what it costs as a fixed price, and who owns the system and data once it's built. A consultant who can't answer those three questions clearly on the first call isn't ready to work with a small business. I'm Jonathan at Uptimize Consulting, based in Naperville, and I answer all three before you've committed to anything.

Search this and you'll get a directory: a handful of Chicago-area firms, some strategy-first, some SMB-focused, most listing a similar set of services. A list of names doesn't tell you which one is right for you. The questions you ask them will.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  • "What specific process would you automate first, and why?" A good answer references something you told them about your business. A vague answer means they haven't actually listened yet.
  • "What's the fixed price and timeline?" If the answer is "it depends" with no follow-up path to an actual number, that's a scoping conversation that hasn't happened yet.
  • "Will I own the system, the code, and the data afterward?" Some consultants build you into a dependency on their platform. You want to know that going in, not after you've paid.
  • "Can you show me one similar project with a real result?" Not a client name if that's sensitive, but a believable before-and-after: hours saved, errors reduced, something concrete.

Red flags

Watch for scope that stays vague with no fixed price attached, a pitch that jumps straight to a large platform before anyone has asked what your actual bottleneck is, and no clear answer about ownership once the engagement ends. Any one of these on its own isn't disqualifying, but two or three together usually means the project will cost more and take longer than what you were told.
